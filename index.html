Clone‑Level and Taxonomic‑Level Identification of Poplar Hybrids Using UAV Hyperspectral Data and Machine Learning

In precision forestry, identifying specific poplar hybrids is essential for monitoring growth and selecting high‑yield genotypes. However, these hybrids—specifically D×M, D×D, and D×T groups—exhibit extreme visual and spectral similarity. Traditional field identification is slow and error‑prone, while raw hyperspectral data contains over 200 bands, many of which are redundant. This "curse of dimensionality" can degrade machine learning accuracy and complicate the extraction of pure canopy signals from background "noise" like soil, shadows, and grass.

Develop a high‑accuracy classification pipeline to differentiate poplar hybrids at both the Taxonomic (group) and Clone (genotype) levels while optimizing spectral feature selection and improving the purity of extracted canopy signals.

  • Preprocessing & Signal Purity: Developed an NDVI‑based masking and crown‑level filtering logic to isolate vegetation pixels and remove background contamination (soil/shadows).
  • Dimensionality Reduction: Compared PCA against the VSURF algorithm to identify the most significant spectral bands, particularly in the Blue and Red‑Edge regions.
  • Machine Learning: Evaluated Random Forest (RF) and Support Vector Machines (SVM) using tree‑level cross‑validation and majority voting to ensure robust spatial predictions.
  • Automation: Designed the workflow for future integration into a custom ArcGIS Pro Python toolbox for scalable, repeatable pixel extraction.
ArcGIS Pro (ArcPy) Python Scikit‑learn NumPy Matplotlib Random Forest SVM PCA VSURF UAV Hyperspectral

Achieved a high Overall Accuracy (OA) of 89.47% and a Kappa coefficient of 0.84 for Taxonomic‑level identification using the first six PCA components. At the more challenging Clone level, the combination of VSURF and RF/SVM yielded an accuracy of 34.41%, highlighting the extreme spectral similarity between individual genotypes. Key findings identified specific influential bands (particularly in the Blue and Red‑Edge regions, such as bands 90, 117, and 144) as critical for discrimination.

Denoising UAV‑Borne Hyperspectral Imagery: A Deep Learning Approach

High‑resolution UAV hyperspectral sensors are revolutionary for precision forestry, but they often produce "noisy" data due to optical sensor imperfections and complex atmospheric interference. This volatility masks critical biological signals, making it difficult to accurately model essential tree health traits like nitrogen or chlorophyll levels at the canopy scale.

Develop a deep learning‑based Denoising Autoencoder (DAE) to programmatically clean 270‑band hyperspectral signals, significantly increasing the Signal‑to‑Noise Ratio (SNR) while preserving high‑fidelity vegetation features.

  • Preprocessing: Used NDVI‑based masking to isolate vegetation pixels and performed data normalization to prepare 270 spectral bands for modeling.
  • Architecture Design: Engineered a five‑layer DAE structure (Encoder‑Decoder) in Python to reconstruct clean spectral signatures from noisy inputs.
  • Optimization: Fine‑tuned the model's latent space representation and reconstruction loss to ensure that biological features remained intact during the cleaning process.
  • Validation: Compared denoised outputs against ground‑truth lab measurements and identified specific spectral intervals that showed the most reliable SNR improvements.
Python Keras TensorFlow NumPy SciPy Autoencoder (DAE) UAV Hyperspectral (270 bands)

The DAE successfully cleaned noise across the entire spectrum, with the most stable improvements found in the Red‑edge to NIR region (700–900 nm). By using NDVI‑restricted denoising, the model provided a much "cleaner" vegetative signal, directly reducing errors in downstream trait‑prediction models.
